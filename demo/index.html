The lighthouse that learned to blink

The lighthouse had stood on the headland for ninety years before anyone noticed it was counting. Every evening, as the sun went down, it flashed a sequence that matched no chart in the harbourmaster's office. Ships' crews assumed it was a fault in the lamp, and wrote it off.

A retired teacher who had moved into the cottage below the cliff began keeping a notebook. She recorded each evening's flashes on the kitchen table, in pencil, next to a cold cup of tea. After four months she had filled a hundred and forty pages and had still not found the pattern.

What she found instead was a name. Buried in the rhythm of the light was a sequence that matched, letter for letter, the christening record of a boat launched in 1887 and lost with all hands in the same winter storm that had nearly taken the lighthouse with it.
